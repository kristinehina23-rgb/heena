# 4. Research Contents and Thesis Structure

**Study.** Recontextualization of Chinese-Language Teaching Materials and Teacher Agency among Pakistani Teachers of Chinese: A Qualitative Study from an Ecological Perspective

The thesis has seven chapters. Two findings chapters are kept separate because the title names two objects: recontextualization of materials, and teacher agency. Localization is the field’s name for the process and outcome when that work orients materials toward a Pakistani classroom. It is not a third theory chapter.

---

## (1) Research Contents

Chapter 1 introduces the problem and keeps the study question-driven. It locates Chinese-language education in Pakistani universities, where imported and HSK-oriented materials travel under syllabus and examination authority, and where localization is usually discussed as a future book. It states the problem that this product story cannot see: teachers already work on the page in class, or follow it tightly, and that work has not been observed. It states the aim, the three research questions, the object and its limits, and the working terms. The object is classroom recontextualization by Pakistani teachers of Chinese, and the agency achieved in that work. The object is not a new national textbook, not an HSK-fit score, and not a test of learner outcomes. The chapter ends by mapping the rest of the thesis.

Chapter 2 reviews the research conversations that could explain that object, and shows where they have not been joined. It is organised by topic, not by country as “abroad / home,” because on a Chinese form “home” usually means China and would scramble a Pakistan site. The topics are materials recontextualization, ecological teacher agency, localization in international Chinese-language education, and Chinese-language education in Pakistani universities. Each topic names what the literature can show and what it cannot. The chapter does not assemble the working model. It ends in the gap: ecological agency in prescribed-textbook settings and the recontextualization of imported materials have been studied as largely separate conversations, and neither has produced an empirically grounded account of how Pakistani teachers of Chinese recontextualize Chinese-language teaching materials in class, with observation plus materials comparison.

Chapter 3 turns that gap into one working model. Recontextualization is operationalized as the pedagogic mechanism visible in materials-in-use: official, teacher, and interactional layers. Ecological agency is operationalized as the achievement of iterational, projective, and practical-evaluative judgement in nested conditions. Materials-in-use is the empirical window, not a third theory. Localization remains the field’s problem-name, not a third box in the figure. The chapter maps the three research questions onto that model and supplies the figure used in later analysis. It does not repeat the literature review.

Chapter 4 reports the design that can see practice rather than opinion alone. It justifies an interpretive qualitative multiple-case study of Pakistani teachers of Chinese nested in institutions. It specifies setting, purposive sampling, and data generation: materials comparison, lesson observation, lesson-focused interviews, stimulated recall where permitted, and institutional field notes. It states how moves, reasons, and agency will be coded, within-case then cross-case. It addresses positionality, interview languages, ethics, and trustworthiness. It refuses a survey of localization beliefs and refuses HSK product analysis as the main method, because those designs already exist in Pakistan CFL writing and cannot answer the questions.

Chapter 5 answers Research Question 1. It reports what teachers actually do with the materials in observed lessons. Cases are organised by practice, not by university as a locale list: coursebook-led transmission, addition and supplementation, deletion and reordering, linguistic mediation, cultural filtering, and teacher-made materials. Faithful transmission is reported as a practice, not written off as a lack of agency. A cross-case pattern is drawn only from these data. Analogues from other countries are not treated as findings.

Chapter 6 answers Research Questions 2 and 3, using the same teachers and a different analytic cut. It reports how teachers justify decisions, how agency is achieved, restricted, or redirected across the triad, and which nested conditions open or close space for manoeuvre. Classroom, collegial and institutional, examination and HSK, professional, and sociocultural conditions are read as part of the achievement, not as background. Forms such as restricted or addition-only work are named only if the cases support them. The chapter ends with one empirical figure: conditions, space for manoeuvre, and recontextualization practices.

Chapter 7 returns to the title with the evidence of Chapters 5 and 6. It argues that, in this setting, localization is teacher-mediated recontextualization under ecological conditions, not textbook production. It places the cases in dialogue with materials-in-use research, ecological agency research, CFL localization research, and Pakistan CFL programme studies. It states theoretical and practical implications, including teacher education and institutional conditions. It does not recommend a new national book as the automatic solution. It states limits of sample, access, and literature coverage, and it names further research, including learner co-recontextualization, which this study does not take as its object.

No fourth-level heading is used. Chapter 2 follows Pattern 2 (topics). Pakistan is one topic, not the whole review. Chapters 5 and 6 stay separate so that 再语境化 and 教师能动性 each have empirical space. The working model sits in Chapter 3, not in Chapter 2. Questionnaires and HSK product evaluation do not become later chapters.
