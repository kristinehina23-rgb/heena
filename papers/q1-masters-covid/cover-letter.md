Dear Editor,

Please consider "When Chinese class moved home: Pakistani students, infrastructure, and platform access" for publication in *Language, Culture and Curriculum*.

This is a new original questionnaire study (N = 38; Wenjuan export, February 2022). It is not a republication. The authors are Heena Rathore (corresponding author), Cao Huimin, and Xu Yiru. Emergency remote Chinese along the Pakistan-China scholarship route failed as a home and platform-access problem. Campus apps (QQ, Chaoxing) often did not open offshore, while Zoom and WhatsApp did. Home conditions were associated with very high stress (OR = 25.67, 95% CI [4.42, 149.00]). Internet speed tracked learning appraisal (ρ = .65).

The manuscript is written for this journal's aims: additional-language learning outside the classroom, identity and investment, and curriculum implementation. The journal has already published international students of Chinese in Chinese universities (Gao & Hua, 2021), campus study-abroad Chinese (Gong, Gao, Li, & Lai, 2020), a designed online Chinese-culture course (Guenier, Xing, & Yan, 2026), CSL learner emotion (Wen & Liu, 2025), students' experience of an institutionalised language curriculum (Shan & Xu, 2024), and the investment agenda for international students of languages other than English (Hajar & Karakuş, 2024). This article supplies a missing South-South case: Chinese-university language classes continued from Pakistani homes, where the campus login often did not open.

Prior note. Rathore and Cao (2024, *Arbor*) is a short preliminary report of the same project. Its printed tables used a larger N (97) that cannot be reconstructed from the only coded export the authors retain. The present article analyses that export in full (N = 38), does not reuse the 2024 tables, and includes sensitivity checks (n = 37 Pakistani; n = 29 offshore IP). We can send the 2024 PDF to the office.

The manuscript is not under review elsewhere. All listed authors have read and approved the submission.
