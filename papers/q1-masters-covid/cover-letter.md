Dear Editor,

Please consider the attached manuscript, “Emergency remote Chinese learning under digital inequality: Pakistani scholarship students, home ecology, and language investment,” for publication in *Language, Culture and Curriculum*.

The paper reports a mixed-methods study of 97 Pakistani students who were enrolled in Chinese universities but took Chinese-language classes from Pakistan during COVID-19. We argue that emergency remote teaching in this corridor failed less as a platform problem than as a **material–context** problem: load-shedding, domestic space, economic strain, time zone, and low platform literacy structured students’ **investment** in Chinese. The study speaks to the journal’s concern with how language curricula travel across cultural and infrastructural settings, and to a still thin literature on Chinese (rather than English) as the medium of South–South educational mobility.

**Relationship to prior publication.** A short preliminary report of this dataset appeared as Rathore and Cao (2024) in *Arbor*. That article was an eight-page exploratory note. The present manuscript is a theoretically reconstructed analysis. It (i) frames the findings through van Dijk’s sequential digital inequality and Norton’s model of investment, (ii) corrects descriptive percentages, (iii) reorganises the qualitative material into a joint display of home ecology and skill outcomes, and (iv) draws implications for International Chinese Language Education after the emergency. We do not reproduce the earlier tables or prose. We would be glad to provide the 2024 PDF to the editorial office.

The manuscript is original, is not under review elsewhere, and all authors have approved the submission. Data were collected with informed consent from adult university students.
