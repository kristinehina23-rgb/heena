Campus-side Chinese platforms (QQ, Chaoxing, DingTalk, MOOC) are the ones students mark as **unusable from home**. Consumer tools that work in Pakistan (Zoom, WhatsApp) are the ones they mark as **available**. ERT here is a **platform-geography** problem, not only a bandwidth problem.

## Table 7. Associations (primary tests)

Small n: report Fisher’s exact for 2×2 and Spearman for ordinals. Logistic models are exploratory (wide CIs) and are in `analysis/results.json` only if needed later.

| Test | Result |
|---|---|
| Home affects study “very much” × very high stress | Fisher *p* < .001, OR = 25.7 (11/14 vs 3/24) |
| Female × “online cannot replace F2F” | Fisher *p* = .029, OR for *male* saying it *can* replace = 7.65 (women 9/11 cannot replace; men 10/27 cannot) |
| Learned little/nothing × cannot replace | Fisher *p* = .029 |
| Poor internet (speed 4–5) × cannot replace | Fisher *p* = .063 (marginal) |
| Internet speed (worse) × learned less | Spearman ρ = .46, *p* = .004 |
| Speed (worse) × less improvement | ρ = .64, *p* < .001 |
| Speed (worse) × less interest | ρ = .59, *p* < .001 |
| Speed (worse) × less teacher interaction | ρ = .55, *p* < .001 |
| Speed (worse) × lower time satisfaction | ρ = .60, *p* < .001 |
| Home-affects-very × stress score (1 = highest stress) | ρ = −.66, *p* < .001 |
| Gender differences on ordinal outcomes | all Mann–Whitney *p* > .10 except as in the 2×2 above |

**Reading.** Worse infrastructure tracks worse learning appraisal and less interaction. A crowded/affecting home tracks very high stress. Women in this sample more often refuse the claim that online Chinese can replace the campus. n = 38: treat *p* values as evidence of association in this file, not as population parameters.
