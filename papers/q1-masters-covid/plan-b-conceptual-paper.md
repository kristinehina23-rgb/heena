## Claim (one sentence)

Localisation of Chinese materials in Pakistan is not translation into Urdu or the addition of local names; it is **evidence-informed fit** between *this component of this book*, *these users’ repertoires and purposes*, and *this programme’s hours, status, assessment, and staffing*.

## What the paper is not

- Not a new textbook.
- Not a learning-gain study.
- Not a Spring Festival paper (already published, 2026, ICCC).
- Not “Pakistan is one locale.” Universities differ.

## Structure (~7,000 words)

1. **Problem.** *HSK Standard Course* travels as a default in many Pakistani programmes. Fit is assumed. Lu et al. (2024) and Lu and Hanif (2025) show teacher/learner friction, mostly single-site or diagnostic. Missing: a model that tells a programme what to **keep, change, or add**.
2. **Localisation debates in ICLE.** Wu (2013) 本土化/普适性; Zhou and Chen (2013); Pym; Jiménez-Crespo; Tomlinson’s materials cycle.
3. **Materials-in-use.** Guerrettaz et al.; Harwood; why “the book” is not the curriculum.
4. **Intercultural and multilingual mediation.** Liddicoat and Scarino; translanguaging as *support* (García and Li Wei; Zhao 2024), not as the whole theory. Urdu is not an automatic solution.
5. **The model.** Figure: Layer A materials → Layer B users → Layer C programme ecology → decision: retain / adapt / supplement / (replace only if misfit is structural). Five dimensions of fit.
6. **Worked example (desk analysis, no new fieldwork).** One unit of *HSK Standard Course* read against two hypothetical but documented programme types (e.g. degree Chinese vs optional CI hours). Show how the same page yields different decisions.
7. **Research agenda.** The multiple-case design already in your Chapter 3/4 plan: 2 Pakistani universities, questionnaires, interviews, component analysis. This section *previews* the PhD; it does not pretend the data exist.
8. **Conclusion.** Fit as the unit of localisation.

## First paragraph you can reuse in 开题

International Chinese teaching materials are often called “localised” when they are translated, decorated with local photographs, or paired with a festival lesson. That usage is too thin for Pakistani universities. Programmes differ in hours, course status, assessment, staffing, and the languages teachers actually use to explain the book. Localisation, in this project, means **material–context fit**: whether a given component of the *HSK Standard Course* (and related resources) can be used by identifiable learners and teachers under those conditions. The output is not a new textbook. It is a set of **retain, adapt, or supplement** decisions that can be evidenced.
