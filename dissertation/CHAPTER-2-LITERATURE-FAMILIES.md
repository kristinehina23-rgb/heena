## Family C — Localization (feeds 2.3.1–2.3.4; not Framework 3)

**Section claim:** Localization is the name the field already uses. In this study it is the process and outcome when recontextualization orients materials toward a Pakistani classroom — not a pre-set goal, not a “better lesson” test, not only a new book. Translanguaging is one mediating resource, not automatically localizing. Comparative cases are analogues, not templates.

## Working claims for later chapters (not hypotheses)

These are sensitizing expectations for observation and coding. They can be revised or dropped. They are **not** Chapter 2 findings and **not** statistical H1/H2.

1. *HSK Standard Course* (or another HSK-oriented series) will often function as the de facto curriculum.
2. Mismatch (language, culture, exam, class size, medium of instruction) will appear as a starting condition teachers already live with, not as the study’s result.
3. Visible moves will include addition, omission, translation/translanguaging, and faithful coverage; a busy adaptation record may still leave the book’s sequence intact.
4. Agency will often look restricted or addition-only under syllabus, HSK, and workload; that still counts as agency.
5. Urdu, English, and regional languages will appear as practical-evaluative resources; they will not by themselves equal localization.
6. Projective purposes will be mixed (exam, order, professional standing, local relevance). Localization will not be a safe default “goal.”

Chapter 3 operationalizes how to see these. Chapter 4 must be able to see practice, not only opinion.
