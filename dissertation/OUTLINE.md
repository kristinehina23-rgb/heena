## 0. What the title now requires

The previous draft treated **localization mainly as material–context fit**: how well HSK and related materials match Pakistani universities, and what localization priorities follow from that evaluation.

The new title moves the object of study:

| Old centre | New centre |
|---|---|
| Textbook / materials as objects to be evaluated | Teachers as agents who transform materials in class |
| Localization as production or design of a local textbook | Localization as everyday classroom work |
| Fit, mismatch, and priorities for revision | **Recontextualization** (what is moved, changed, silenced, or added) |
| Locale as a set of conditions “out there” | **Ecological teacher agency**: conditions become resources and constraints for action |
| Mixed emphasis on analysis of materials | Qualitative study of practice, reasoning, and context |

Keep the Pakistan / CFL / HSK-materials setting. Do **not** keep “five dimensions of fit” as the main research object. Those dimensions become **ecological conditions** that teachers read, work with, or work around.

The study’s claim, in one sentence:

> Localization of Chinese-language teaching in Pakistan is not first a textbook-writing problem; it is a teacher-mediated achievement, produced when Pakistani teachers of Chinese recontextualize materials under specific ecological conditions of possibility and constraint.

## 1. Working abstract (locked to the title)

This study examines how Pakistani teachers of Chinese recontextualize Chinese-language teaching materials in their classroom practices. Rather than treating localization solely as a matter of textbook production, it investigates the institutional, pedagogical, and professional conditions that shape teachers’ decisions about what to adapt, how to adapt it, and why. The study aims to demonstrate how localization is enacted through everyday teaching practices and to provide an empirically grounded account of teacher-mediated localization. In doing so, it helps researchers and educators understand the classroom as a site where localization is actively produced rather than merely received.

---

## 2. Working research questions

**Overarching question**  
How do Pakistani teachers of Chinese recontextualize Chinese-language teaching materials in classroom practice, and how is their agency achieved or constrained within the ecological conditions of their work?

**RQ1 — Practices (what / how)**  
What recontextualization practices do Pakistani teachers of Chinese enact when using Chinese-language teaching materials (addition, omission, substitution, modification, reordering, supplementation, translanguaging, cultural filtering, or faithful transmission)?

**RQ2 — Reasoning (why)**  
How do teachers justify these decisions in relation to learners, materials, pedagogy, culture, language resources (Chinese, Urdu, English, and others), assessment, time, and professional identity?

**RQ3 — Agency (achievement, not a trait)**  
In what ways is teacher agency achieved, restricted, or redirected across the iterational (past), projective (future), and practical-evaluative (present) dimensions of ecological agency?

**RQ4 — Ecology (conditions)**  
Which nested conditions—classroom, collegial and institutional, examination and HSK policy, professional community, and wider sociocultural setting—enable or constrain teacher-mediated localization?

These four questions map directly onto the English title (recontextualization + teacher agency + ecological perspective + qualitative study) and onto the Chinese title (再语境化 + 教师能动性 + 生态视角 + 质性研究).

## 3. Key concepts (use these terms consistently)

**Recontextualization / 再语境化**  
Not a synonym for “localization of a textbook.” It is the selective taking-up, relocation, and transformation of materials and pedagogic knowledge when they are moved into a new classroom context. Teachers decide what travels, what is silenced, what is added from local life, and what is re-ordered for this class, this exam, this university.

**Localization / 本土化**  
In this study, localization is the *process and outcome* of teacher-mediated recontextualization, not only the design of a national or Urdu-medium textbook, and not a pre-set goal or a test of whether the lesson was “better.” The thesis argues that localization is produced in classrooms, or blocked there. “The local” is constructed.

**Teacher agency / 教师能动性**  
Agency is **achieved in context**, not possessed as a personal trait and not a driver teachers “exercise in order to localize” (Biesta & Tedder, 2007; Priestley et al., 2012, 2016). A teacher may have strong beliefs and still have little space for manoeuvre. Restricted or addition-only agency still counts.

**How the three relate (locked)**  
Recontextualization is the pedagogic mechanism visible in materials-in-use. Ecological agency is the achievement that makes that mechanism possible, restricted, or redirected. Localization is the name the Pakistan conversation already uses, and in this study the process and outcome when recontextualization orients materials toward a Pakistani classroom. Do **not** write: agency (driver) → localization (goal) → recontextualization (practice). “Why teachers do it” is practical-evaluative reasoning (RQ2), not a synonym for agency.

**Ecological perspective / 生态视角**  
Agency arises from the interplay of:

- **iterational** resources (biography, training, prior materials use)
- **projective** purposes (what kind of teacher / learner / Chinese class they want)
- **practical-evaluative** judgements in the present (time, exams, students, syllabus, colleagues)
- nested settings: classroom (micro), department/university (meso), HSK/policy/profession (exo–macro)

**Materials-in-use / 教材使用**  
Materials are not only texts to be evaluated. In class they become the de facto curriculum, interactional resources, and sometimes a constraint that de-professionalizes teachers (Guerrettaz & Johnston, 2013; Song, 2024).

**Pakistani teachers of Chinese / 巴基斯坦本土中文教师**  
Local teachers (and, if sampled, Chinese teachers working in Pakistan) who teach Chinese in Pakistani institutions. The title centres **teachers**, not the HSK book as a text.

## Chapter 1  Introduction / 绪论

### 1.1 Research background
- Growth of Chinese-language education in Pakistan (CPEC, Confucius Institutes / Classroom programmes, university Chinese majors and optional courses).
- Dominance of imported or nationally compiled Chinese materials (especially HSK-oriented series) designed for a general international learner, not for Pakistani classrooms.
- Public and academic talk about “localization” usually points to **new textbooks**, Urdu glosses, or national compilation. Classroom teachers disappear from that story.

### 1.2 Problem statement
- Materials do not enter Pakistani classrooms as finished localization.
- Teachers already add, cut, translate, skip culture, insert local examples, teach to HSK, or follow the book tightly.
- Existing CFL work in Pakistan has described materials, programmes, and learner/teacher perceptions of fit. It has **not** given a thick account of how local Chinese teachers recontextualize materials, nor of how their agency is achieved or blocked.
- Treating localization only as textbook production misrecognizes the classroom as the site where localization is actually made.

### 1.3 Aim and significance
- Aim: an empirically grounded account of teacher-mediated localization.
- Theoretical significance: join **recontextualization**, **ecological teacher agency**, and **materials-in-use** in CFL, a combination still rare outside EFL.
- Practical significance: teacher education, material adaptation guidance, and institutional conditions (time, exams, collegial support) rather than only “write a Pakistani textbook.”
- Contextual significance: Pakistan as a multilingual, examination-shaped, resource-uneven CFL setting.

### 1.4 Research questions  
Paste the four RQs from §2.

### 1.5 Object, scope, and delimitations
- **Object:** classroom recontextualization of Chinese-language teaching materials by Pakistani teachers of Chinese, and the agency achieved therein.
- **Not the object:** writing a new national textbook; evaluating HSK 3.0 as a document in isolation; measuring learning outcomes of adapted lessons.
- **Scope:** selected Pakistani universities / institutes (specify later). Materials actually used (HSK Standard Course and/or supplements—name what teachers really use, do not assume one book).
- **Delimitations:** qualitative; teacher-centred (learner voice only as it appears in teachers’ reasoning and observed lessons); no claim that Pakistan is one locale.

### 1.6 Definitions of key terms  
Short working definitions matching §3: recontextualization (mechanism), ecological teacher agency (achievement), localization (process/outcome, not a pre-set goal), ecology, materials-in-use, Pakistani teachers of Chinese. Do not use driver → goal → practice.

## Chapter 3  Theoretical framework / 理论基础与分析框架

**Job of this chapter:** give one working model. Do not repeat Chapter 2.

### 3.1 Why a combined framework
No single theory covers the title. Bernstein-type recontextualization explains **what happens to materials**. Ecological agency explains **how teachers can or cannot do that work**. Materials-in-use explains **where** it is visible (classroom ecology).

### 3.2 Recontextualization of teaching materials
- Pedagogic recontextualization: selection, relocation, delocation, transformation of knowledge and texts for a new pedagogic site (use Bernstein’s pedagogic device in the actual chapter; keep the wording precise and cited from the original, not from secondary slogans).
- Classroom-level recontextualization in language teaching: Yasasri (2024); Ling et al. (2023); Yu (2015); Alvunger (2018) on curriculum recontextualization as teacher agency; Nan et al. (2023) on translanguaging recontextualization in Indonesian Mandarin teaching; Rathert’s adaptation moves as observable traces of recontextualization.
- Distinguish:
  - **official recontextualization** (syllabus, HSK, prescribed book)
  - **teacher recontextualization** (lesson-level transformation)
  - **interactional recontextualization** (what actually happens with students)

### 3.3 Ecological teacher agency
- Agency-as-achievement (Biesta & Tedder, 2007).
- Chordal triad: iterational / projective / practical-evaluative (Emirbayer & Mische as used by Priestley, Biesta & Robinson, 2015).
- Space for manoeuvre (Priestley et al., 2012).
- Forms useful for coding, not as a rigid typology:
  - acceptance, adaptation, resistance (Dao et al., 2025)
  - compliant / persistent agency (Chen & Shu, 2026)
  - constrained / transformative / progressive (Wang, 2022)
  - restricted agency (Tao & Weng, 2025)
- Beliefs as cultural resources that need institutional cultures (Biesta, Priestley & Robinson, 2015).

### 3.4 Nested ecology of Pakistani Chinese teaching
Use a simplified nested map, not a full Bronfenbrenner textbook:

| Layer | Examples in this study |
|---|---|
| Micro | lesson, textbook page, students, languages in class |
| Meso | department, timetable, colleagues, workload, who chooses the book |
| Exo | HSK, university exam rules, Confucius Institute / partner constraints |
| Macro | national language-in-education policy, CPEC discourse, professional status of local Chinese teachers |
| Chrono | teacher biography; programme history; shifting HSK standards |

Namgung et al. (2023) and Tao (2021) support this map. The university is likely a decisive meso filter.

### 3.5 Materials-in-use as the empirical window
- Guerrettaz: materials structure interaction.
- Remillard / Li: participatory / arc models of use (Li & Li, 2021; Mao & Qi, 2026).
- Observable moves: add, delete, modify, simplify, reorder, substitute, supplement, translanguage, culturally filter, transmit unchanged (Rathert; Hanifa; Uri).

## Chapter 4  Methodology / 研究设计

Qualitative study, ecological perspective—this must match the title. Do not drift back into textbook content analysis as the main method. Content analysis of materials is **supporting** evidence only (what was available to be recontextualized).

### 4.1 Research paradigm
Interpretive qualitative; practice-oriented; teacher as meaning-maker in context.

### 4.2 Design
Multiple-case qualitative study of Pakistani teachers of Chinese (individual teachers as cases nested in institutions). Not a large-N survey. Yan & Weng (2026), Dao et al. (2025), Bao et al. (2020), Hanifa et al. (2024) are design analogues.

### 4.3 Setting and participants
- Pakistani universities / institutes where Chinese is taught.
- Purposive sampling: local Pakistani teachers of Chinese; variation in experience, institution type, materials used, HSK orientation.
- If Chinese national teachers are included, treat them as a **contrastive** case, not as the title population.
- Sample size: quality of observation + interview depth over number (justify with saturation and case logic).

### 4.4 Data generation (must be able to see practice, not only opinion)

| Source | What it shows | Link to RQs |
|---|---|---|
| Semi-structured interviews (life-history + lesson-focused) | iterational and projective dimensions; why | RQ2, RQ3, RQ4 |
| Lesson observation / video if permitted | actual recontextualization | RQ1, RQ3 |
| Stimulated recall after observed lessons | in-the-moment practical-evaluative judgement | RQ2, RQ3 |
| Documents: textbook pages, slides, worksheets, tests, teacher-made materials | traces of addition/deletion/substitution | RQ1 |

- Stage 3: agency coding with the chordal triad + forms (restricted, compliant, persistent, transformative).

### 4.6 Researcher positionality, language, and ethics

- Languages of interview (Urdu, English, Chinese) as data, not noise.

### 4.7 Trustworthiness

## Chapter 5  Findings I: Recontextualization practices / 研究发现一：教材再语境化实践

Answer **RQ1** (and the “what/how” part of the abstract). Organize by **practice**, with case vignettes, not by university-as-locale list.

### 5.1 Overview of cases and materials actually used
Table of teachers, institutions (anonymized), courses, prescribed books, supplements. This prevents assuming everyone uses one HSK volume.

### 5.2 Transmission and coursebook-led use
When the book remains the de facto curriculum (Guerrettaz & Johnston, 2013; Rathert, 2021). Faithful coverage as a form of agency under exam pressure—not automatically “lack of agency.”

### 5.3 Additive and supplementary recontextualization
Local examples, extra drills, digital materials, teacher-made worksheets, Pakistani daily life as content (Zhao, 2020; Hanifa et al., 2024; Uri, 2024). Yan & Weng’s “we can only do addition” as a comparative motif.

### 5.4 Deletion, simplification, reordering
Skipping culture notes, cutting tasks, changing sequence to match university calendar or HSK test order.

### 5.5 Linguistic recontextualization: translanguaging and mediation
Urdu / English / Chinese / other languages as resources (Huang et al., 2025; Han, 2022; Zhang et al., 2025). How teachers open or close translanguaging spaces.

### 5.6 Cultural filtering and local meaning-making
What Chinese cultural content is kept, censored, replaced, or story-ized (Gray, 2000; Lee, 2020; Yasasri, 2024). Watch Vu & Pham’s warning: language may be adapted while ideology is left untouched.

### 5.7 Teachers as designers, not only users
Teacher-produced materials, slides, tests (Li, Gao & Cui, 2021; Yang et al., 2024). Where design is rich, and where it is emergency patching.

### 5.8 Cross-case pattern of practices
A typology **emerging from data**, which may be compared with—not copied from—Zhang et al. (2022) profiles or Shawer’s transmitter/maker/developer.

### 5.9 Chapter summary
What localization looks like **as classroom production**.

## Chapter 6  Findings II: Teacher agency in ecological conditions / 研究发现二：生态条件下的教师能动性

Answer **RQ2–RQ4**. Same teachers, different analytic cut.

### 6.1 Iterational dimension
Training (China / Pakistan), years of teaching, prior textbooks, professional biography. How past experience becomes a resource or a rut.

### 6.2 Projective dimension
Imagined students, imagined professional identity (local expert, HSK coach, cultural mediator), hoped-for Chinese programme. Liyanage & Walker (2023); Zhang, Li & Sang (2025).

### 6.3 Practical-evaluative dimension
In-the-moment judgements: this class, this week, this exam, this page. Time, mixed proficiency, attendance, “finish the book.”

### 6.4 Classroom and material conditions (micro)
Affordances and limits of the prescribed Chinese materials; physical classroom; student response.

### 6.5 Collegial and institutional conditions (meso)
Who selects materials; inspection; workload; whether colleagues share adaptations; university as filter (Tao, 2021; Biesta et al., 2015 on professional discourse).

### 6.6 Assessment, HSK, and policy conditions (exo–macro)
Output regulation (Priestley et al., 2016); HSK as both resource and shackle (Tao & Weng, 2025). Policy talk of localization versus classroom space for manoeuvre.

### 6.7 Forms of agency achieved
Show **variation**: restricted, compliant, persistent, transformative—only if the data support those labels. Include teachers who want to localize but can only add; teachers who resist the book; teachers who strategically comply.

## Chapter 7  Discussion and conclusion / 讨论与结论

### 7.1 Returning to the title
State, with evidence, that localization in this setting is teacher-mediated recontextualization under ecology, not textbook production.

### 7.2 Dialogue with literature
- With materials-use research: Pakistan CFL as a classroom-ecology case.
- With ecological agency: what is distinctive when the subject is Chinese, the teachers are local, and HSK/imported materials dominate.
- With CFL localization research: move the field from “need a local book” to “need conditions in which teachers can recontextualize well.”
- With Pakistan CFL studies: add practice-level evidence that perception-of-fit studies could not see.

### 7.3 Theoretical contribution
A working account of **teacher-mediated localization** as ecological achievement. Clarify recontextualization as the mechanism and agency as the achievement.

### 7.4 Practical implications
- Teacher education: adaptation and recontextualization as professional skill, not deviation.
- Institutions: time, collegial sharing, and assessment rules shape agency more than slogans about 本土化.
- Materials developers: design for **use and adaptation**, not only coverage.
- Do not recommend “replace HSK with a national book” as the automatic solution unless the data say so.

### 7.5 Limitations
Sample, access to classrooms, English-heavy literature base until CNKI is complete, papers 221–240 missing from the last evidence table, no learner-outcome measures.

### 7.6 Further research
Learner co-recontextualization; Chinese vs Pakistani teachers in the same department; longitudinal agency; digital/AI materials (Lin et al., 2025 only if relevant).

### 7.7 Conclusion
Close on the abstract’s last sentence: the classroom is where localization is produced, not merely received.
