# Literature Review for the PhD Proposal

**Study.** Recontextualization of Chinese-Language Teaching Materials and Teacher Agency among Pakistani Teachers of Chinese: A Qualitative Study from an Ecological Perspective.

This review is written for the proposal. It is not a tour of the 240-item search archive. About forty-five books and papers are used here because they can support, or threaten, the claim that localization in Pakistani Chinese-language teaching is produced in class, or blocked there. The larger archive remains a search record. It is not a claim that every PDF has been read. A CNKI search is still required. C. Wang (2022) is identified from citing literature; that dissertation PDF was not available here.

The review is organised by conversation, not by author. Each conversation is read for what its typical method can show, and for what it cannot show. Related publications from one programme are counted once.

---

## 1.  Focus of the review

The proposal has one core question. How do Pakistani teachers of Chinese recontextualize Chinese-language teaching materials in classroom practice, and how is their agency achieved or constrained within the ecological conditions of their work?

Four smaller questions follow from that core. What practices are enacted, including faithful transmission. How teachers justify those decisions. How agency is achieved, restricted, or redirected across iterational, projective, and practical-evaluative dimensions. Which nested conditions enable or constrain teacher-mediated localization.

The literature cannot be asked to answer those questions until three terms are kept apart. Recontextualization is the pedagogic mechanism visible in materials-in-use (Bernstein, 2000). Ecological teacher agency is the achievement that makes that mechanism possible, restricted, or redirected (Biesta & Tedder, 2007; Priestley, Biesta, & Robinson, 2015). Localization is the name the Pakistan conversation already uses. In this study it is the process and outcome when recontextualization orients materials toward a Pakistani classroom. It is not a third theoretical framework. It is not a pre-set goal. It is not a test of whether the lesson was better.

If those terms collapse, the gap disappears for the wrong reason. Adaptation lists will look like an answer to the first question. Belief surveys will look like an answer to the third. Calls for an Urdu-enriched HSK edition will look like an answer to localization. None of those is the missing account.

---

## 2.  What happens to materials

Bernstein (2000) is the origin of recontextualization in this proposal. Knowledge is not delivered intact from a field of production into a classroom. It is selectively appropriated, relocated, and refocused as pedagogic communication. An official recontextualizing field of syllabuses, inspectors, and examination authorities sets terms. A pedagogic recontextualizing field of publishers, faculties, and advisers mediates those terms. Evaluative rules feed back into what may be selected. An HSK-oriented series already arrives as such an object. The classroom is a further site, not the first one.

Language-education research has used the word more loosely. Yu (2015) treats interpretation, re-interpretation, and recontextualization as what university teachers in China do with central reform. That usage is close to practice. It also lets the term mean any local handling of a text from elsewhere. Follow that looser sense and this proposal becomes a study of implementation. The materials disappear.

Empirical materials research split after that concept, and the split is methodological.

One strand studies the book in the classroom ecology. Guerrettaz and Johnston (2013) observed an intact language class. The coursebook became the de facto curriculum. It organised turns, topics, and legitimate departures from the page. Guerrettaz et al. (2022) and Harwood (2021) recast that finding as an agenda: materials as content, as consumption, and as production. Product evaluation cannot, on that agenda, explain what a book does educationally. Z. Li (2020) and Li and Xu (2020) make the same point from Chinese higher-education English classrooms: use is a process, not a reading of the page. The limit of this strand is the population and the language object. These are not studies of Pakistani teachers of Chinese working with HSK-oriented series.

A second strand studies adaptation as professional work. Rathert and Cabaroğlu (2022) treat adaptation as a principled response to mismatch between a generalised book and a particular class. Rathert and Cabaroğlu (2021), in an in-depth study of two English teachers, show the other end of the same problem: teachers can remain coursebook-led when conditions restrict what they can change. Hanifa and Yusra (2023), with the follow-on analysis in Hanifa et al. (2024), show addition, omission, substitution, and local cultural substitution under time, resource, and examination pressure in Acehnese and Indonesian English classrooms. The two papers are one programme. Mohamad Uri and Abdul Aziz (2024) add scale in Malaysia: imported CEFR books raise workload. Adaptation is labour, not preference. Gray (2000) and Song (2024) add a further caution. Teachers censor as well as adapt. Following the book can be a professional stance, rather than a failure of imagination.

Criado (2023) is the limit that this proposal has to keep. In EFL textbook use, teachers altered a large share of activities while remaining largely faithful to sequence. Procedural change even made some lessons more teacher-fronted. Neupane (2025) reports that stated willingness to adapt often exceeds what is done. A move-count can therefore look like localization and still leave the prescribed course as the curriculum. Toledo-Sandoval (2020) shows the same stubbornness from the product side. Even locally produced Chilean ELT books still needed classroom work, because the local on the page and the local of the class were not the same construction.

Alvunger (2018) comes closest to joining Bernstein’s device to teachers’ work. Swedish teachers appear as agents who borrow and combine content rather than as couriers. The setting is not Chinese-language education. The population is not Pakistani. The object is not an HSK-oriented series. It is a near-miss, not a substitute.

What this conversation can show is that materials both organise practice and get worked on. What it cannot show is what knowledge, language, and culture were transformed when Pakistani teachers of Chinese moved an HSK page into a class. Observation of English-textbook use is not that evidence.

## 3.  Whether teachers can act

If recontextualization is the operation, the next question is whether it can be done. Capacity theories treat agency as something a person has. Bandura (2001) is the clearest statement of that family: intentionality, forethought, self-regulation, self-reflectiveness. Beliefs matter. The inference that easily follows does not. If practice looks conservative, the diagnosis becomes that teachers lack agency, as if training could supply a missing trait. An HSK-led university course can leave a committed teacher with almost no room to change the sequence.

The ecological account relocates the problem. Emirbayer and Mische (1998) treat agency as a temporally embedded process: iterational, projective, and practical-evaluative dimensions sounding together. Biesta and Tedder (2007) treat it as an achievement in contexts-for-action. Priestley, Biesta, and Robinson (2015) specify that account for teachers’ work with prescribed curriculum. Autonomy is a condition that may or may not support agency. It is not a synonym for agency (Priestley et al., 2016). Beliefs are cultural resources. They do not suffice without professional discourse in which alternatives can be spoken (Biesta, Priestley, & Robinson, 2015). The 2015 book, the 2015 beliefs article, and Priestley et al. (2012, 2016) are one programme, not four independent confirmations. The Scottish Curriculum for Excellence cases are analogues.

Tao and Gao (2021) map conceptualisations of language teacher agency. They are used here as a map, not as a second working model. This proposal does not take “promote teacher agency” as its aim.

Empirical studies of prescribed textbooks give the ecological definition operational form, and they converge on restriction rather than on heroic change. L. Wang (2022) distinguishes constrained, transformative, and progressive agency among English teachers under curriculum reform in China, and locates the difference in examination pressure and school culture. Yan and Weng (2026) name a pattern that is immediately relevant to an HSK-led course: teachers can often only add. Tao and Weng (2025) describe ideological support with shackled practice. Dao et al. (2025), in a classroom-level case of two Vietnamese English teachers, code acceptance, adaptation, and resistance toward a new textbooks policy. Chen and Shu (2026) distinguish approval, compliant agency, and persistent agency. Bao et al. (2020) is the CFL-adjacent bridge: identity, community, and materials among teachers of Chinese as an additional language in Belarus. It still does not study Pakistani teachers of Chinese, and it does not take an HSK-oriented series as the object of classroom recontextualization.

These studies do not say that teachers have agency. They say that prescribed materials are mediated unevenly, and that restricted work still counts. Interview-reported belief is not enacted mediation. That is a finding of the family, not a slogan. The limit is the same as in the materials family. The close studies are chiefly EFL reform in China and Vietnam. Analogues can later guide coding. They cannot occupy this title.

## 4.  Localization as the field’s name

Localization has no Bernstein genealogy, and this proposal does not invent one. The word is retained because Pakistani policy and scholarship already use it.

Product studies of global Chinese textbooks can show official recontextualization already inscribed in the book. Yasasri (2024), through corpus and critical discourse analysis of global Chinese textbooks used in Sri Lanka, shows exclusion of South Asian cultures and local languages and a one-way cultural adaptation. Dassanayake (2023) converts a related critique into a checklist covering language, culture, pedagogy, cost, and local inclusiveness. Both are valuable. Both remain closer to product evaluation than to classroom practice. They can show what was available to be taught. They cannot show what was taught.

A later strand treats localization as teacher work. Zhao (2020), Zhao (2024a), and Zhao et al. (2024b) are one Australian programme in which students’ everyday practices and funds of knowledge become Chinese-learning content, including through translanguaging. Ling et al. (2023) describe bilingual teachers in Australian schools building from students’ sociocultural worlds rather than only from the book’s China. Loo (2026) is sharper on the institutional point: localization of borrowed global curricula can be left to teachers, weakly supported, reactive, and politically shaped.

Translanguaging sits next to this strand. It is not automatically localizing. Q. Zhang et al. (2020) show coordinated language use supporting explanation and participation in CFL classes despite monolingual norms. D. Wang (2022), in an eighteen-month ethnography in Hong Kong, shows interpretive, managerial, and interactive translanguaging with ethnic-minority learners. Nan et al. (2023) name a “recontextualization of translanguaging” in Indonesian Mandarin teaching. Language choice may be a means of moving a book into a class. It is not the whole operation, and it is not Pakistan data.

Contextualization can still transmit the book’s sequence, examination logic, and cultural hierarchy almost unchanged (Ling et al., 2023). Recontextualization can also delocalize: teaching to the test, stripping culture, replacing local life with a generic international Chinese (Gray, 2000). Localization is one possible bearing of the operation, not its definition.

Pakistan is not in the teacher-work set. Australia, Thailand-adjacent cases, Indonesia, Sri Lanka, and Hong Kong cannot stand in for Pakistani universities.

## 5.  The Pakistani university setting

Pakistan is not an unstudied country in Chinese-language education. It is under-studied in the specific respect this title names.

C. Wang (2022) maps Pakistani Chinese teaching as a programme and international-communication system. Claims from that dissertation remain provisional until the PDF is read. Hanif (2023) documents Confucius Institute expansion, dependence on volunteer teachers, shortage of local experts, and the lack of standardized, locally adequate materials. Khan et al. (2022) show a gap between mandatory Chinese policy at a higher-education institution and classroom reality, especially class size, medium of instruction, and intended outcomes. Azeem et al. (2022) report weak curriculum uniformity, English-annotated textbooks, and the burden placed on teachers to add or delete content. Ali and David (2022) place Chinese in Sindh’s language ecology of English prestige, Urdu nationalism, and regional identity. Naheed (2026) surveys 55 local teachers and reports heavy use of *HSK Standard Course* (the paper’s figure is 78.18%) alongside professional-development needs.

Two closely related publications examine *HSK Standard Course* in Pakistan and must not be counted as independent evidence. Lu et al. (2024) combine a literature review, student questionnaires, and interviews with Pakistani Chinese teachers, and recommend Urdu annotations, cultural comparison, and localized modules. Lu and Hanif (2025) is a follow-on article. Same first author, same corresponding author, same textbook object. It turns the emphasis toward pedagogical challenges and Urdu as support. Hanif (2023) belongs to the same authorial background on Confucius Institutes.

These studies establish landscape and perceived fit. HSK functions here as an institutional condition of teachers’ work, not as a document to be scored in isolation. They do not observe everyday recontextualization. They do not compare the prescribed page with what was actually taught. They propose materials-production remedies. That is a different research object.

## 6.  Methodological comparison

The conversations above do not fail for lack of publication. They fail, for this title, at the join of object, population, and method.

| Conversation | Typical method | Typical population and object | What it can show | What it cannot show for this title |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Materials-in-use | Classroom observation; ecology of the book | ELT / language classes; not Pakistani CFL | The book as de facto curriculum; interaction structured by the page | How Pakistani teachers of Chinese move HSK materials in class |
| Adaptation as professional work | Case study; interview; some observation; survey of practices | ELT in Turkey, Indonesia, Malaysia, Nepal | Moves, coursebook-led versus teacher-led use, workload | Whether a busy move-count transformed knowledge, language, or culture |
| Product / corpus CFL | Textbook analysis; checklist; CDA | Global Chinese books, often Sri Lanka | Official recontextualization already in the book | What was taught |
| Ecological agency with prescribed books | Qualitative case; often interview-heavy; some classroom observation | China and Vietnam EFL reform; Belarus CFL bridge | Restricted, addition-only, compliant, and persistent forms | Pakistani teachers of Chinese; HSK series as the classroom object |
| Pakistan CFL | Programme studies; questionnaires; teacher interviews | Pakistani universities and Confucius Institutes; *HSK Standard Course* | Perceived misfit; HSK as a widely used series; calls for Urdu support | Observation plus materials comparison of everyday recontextualization |

Three comparisons follow from the table, and they are the warrant for the design, not a list of complaints.

First, textbook analysis and classroom observation are not interchangeable evidence of the same thing. Yasasri (2024) and Dassanayake (2023) recover the official layer. Guerrettaz and Johnston (2013) recover the interactional layer. Lu et al. (2024) recover what teachers and students say about fit. None of those, alone, is recontextualization as enacted practice.

Second, interview-reported willingness is a poor proxy for practice. Criado (2023) and Neupane (2025) already show the discrepancy inside ELT. A Pakistan study of “localization beliefs” would reproduce that discrepancy as if it were the finding.

Third, analogues cannot be stacked until they look like this population. Dao et al. (2025) and Yan and Weng (2026) show that observation of textbook-mediated agency is possible. The method exists. The language object and the national setting do not. Alvunger (2018) and Nan et al. (2023) occupy parts of the conceptual join in Sweden and Indonesia. They do not cancel the Pakistan CFL problem.

---

## 7.  Research gap

Previous studies have separately examined ecological teacher agency in the use of prescribed language textbooks, chiefly among EFL teachers in China, Vietnam and related curriculum-reform settings, and the adaptation or recontextualization of imported and global teaching materials, including ELT cases in Malaysia, Indonesia and rural Southeast Asia and CFL textbook or contextualization studies in Sri Lanka, Australia and related sites. Limited empirical research has investigated recontextualization of Chinese-language teaching materials and ecological teacher agency in a single design, and Pakistani teachers of Chinese are almost absent from both literatures. Insufficient attention has been given to classroom observation plus materials comparison that would show how localization is produced in everyday teaching rather than received as textbook production, and to the institutional, pedagogical and professional conditions that shape what Pakistani teachers of Chinese adapt, how they adapt it, and why.

The proposal therefore does not ask whether HSK materials “fit” Pakistan, and it does not offer a new textbook as its product. It asks how Pakistani teachers of Chinese recontextualize the materials they already use, how they justify those decisions, how agency is achieved or restricted in that work, and which nested conditions make the work possible or block it.
